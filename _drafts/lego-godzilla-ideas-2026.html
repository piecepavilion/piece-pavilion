LEGO Godzilla from LEGO Ideas: Two Sets Arriving Black Friday 2026

Piece Pavilion · November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

LEGO just confirmed what AFOLs on both coasts have been whispering about for months: a massive, fan-designed Godzilla set is landing on Black Friday 2026 — and a free giveaway mini-build is riding along with it. Both come straight out of LEGO Ideas, the platform where fans submit concepts and (very occasionally) see them turned into real product.

Set 21375: LEGO Ideas Godzilla

The main event is enormous. At 5,363 pieces, set 21375 is the first LEGO Ideas release to break 5,000 pieces — the largest Ideas set ever produced. Dressed as the Monsterverse version of Godzilla (the one from the 2014 and 2019 movies), the finished model stands about 70 cm (27.6 in) tall and 40 cm (15.7 in) wide, with the Atomic Breath beam attached.

Articulation is where it earns its price tag. The shoulders rotate, the forearms tilt in steps, the wrists and claws move individually, and the head tilts. The tail is locked at build time — you choose left or right — but the swappable detail is the tongue: pop the 4-piece mouth insert out and replace it with a sculpted energy beam for display. The dorsal spikes are a deep translucent blue; they don't light up, but they catch the light nicely.

  • Price: $449.99 / £349.99 / €399.99
  • Release: November 27, 2026 (Black Friday) — LEGO.com exclusive at launch
  • Age: 18+
  • Minifigures: None (it's a monster, not a city)

Set 40888: Godzilla Logo with Footprint (free GWP)

Buy Godzilla at LEGO.com from November 27–30 and you get this one free. It's a small diorama — 359 pieces, 18+ — of a Godzilla footprint pressed into the ground next to a flip-displayable "GODZILLA" word, with a few wrecked buildings in the background and a new light-aqua butterfly element that fans are already reading as a Mothra nod.

How LEGO Ideas actually works

This is the part Dad gets asked about all the time: fan designs don't just show up on the shelf. LEGO Ideas is a crowdsourced pipeline. You upload your build to ideas.lego.com, then you spend months rallying people to click "Support". If you hit 10,000 supporters inside the clock window, your project graduates to the Review Stage, where a board of LEGO set designers, product managers, and marketing folks decide if it becomes a real product. Most don't make it.

If LEGO greenlights it, the fan designer typically gets their name on the box and a share of net sales — the long-standing public figure is 1% of net revenue plus free copies of the finished set. (LEGO has been quieter about the exact number in its newer help pages, so treat that as the traditional baseline rather than a current legal guarantee.)

Meet the designer: MattE720

The fan behind Godzilla is Matthew Esposito, who goes by MattE720 on LEGO Ideas. He's based in Long Island, New York. He posted the project in December 2023 after roughly four to five months of designing, it crossed 10,000 supporters, and in June 2025 it came out of the third 2024 LEGO Ideas Review as a confirmed "yes". A little over a year later, here it is in your cart.

That timeline — 12 to 18 months from "greenlit" to "shipping" — is typical. If you've ever wondered why your favorite Ideas concept takes forever to materialize, now you know.
