LEGO Advent Calendars 2026, Ranked: Which One Is Actually Worth It

Piece Pavilion · October 26, 2026

December 1 is five weeks away. LEGO Advent Calendars dropped September 1, and if you have not grabbed one yet, you are already starting to feel the clock. Here is the thing nobody tells you: the Star Wars calendar sells out at the LEGO store by mid-November every single year, and this year the lineup is smaller than ever — no Harry Potter, no Minecraft, just five calendars across the whole catalog.

We ranked them. Not by piece count. Not by price. By what you will actually keep after December 25 — which, for a BrickLink store that watches these minifigs flood the secondary market every January, is the only metric that matters.

The 2026 lineup at a glance

  • Star Wars (75456) — The Mandalorian, 293 pieces, $44.99
  • Marvel (76340) — Six superhero minifigs, 317 pieces, $44.99
  • City (60510) — Santa's Workshop, 252 pieces
  • Disney Princess (43298) — Four minidolls, 290 pieces
  • Friends (42698) — Winter vignette, around $27–$35

Notable absences: no Harry Potter calendar for the first time since 2019, and no Minecraft calendar after it was the breakout star of 2025. If either theme is on your gift list, you are shopping the secondary market for a previous-year calendar — or buying the loose minifigs as singles.

The ranking

1

LEGO Star Wars Advent Calendar — The Mandalorian

Set 75456
📦 293 pieces 💵 $44.99 👤 5 minifigs + 2 figures ⏱ Ages 6+

What you get: The Mandalorian, Moff Gideon, Carson Teva, a Jawa, and IG-11 — all in holiday variants — plus Grogu and a Pit Droid as small figures. Four creature figures (Blurrg, Mudhorn, Bantha, Dewback). And seven mini-build vehicles including Mando's N-1 Starfighter, the Razor Crest, a Sandcrawler, an Outland TIE Fighter, an Imperial Light Cruiser, a speeder bike, and a festive X-wing.

That is a lot of calendar. Five full minifigs matches last year's Marvel count, every single one is a named character from The Mandalorian, and holiday-variant Moff Gideon and IG-11 are figures that do not exist in any other set. The seven vehicles are the mini-build sweet spot — buildable in a minute, actually displayable on a shelf, and all seven together make a complete Mando-universe diorama.

Verdict: The one to grab. Highest minifig count, strongest IP, best resale after Christmas. If you buy only one calendar this year, buy this one. Expect it to sell out at LEGO by mid-November.
2

LEGO Marvel Advent Calendar

Set 76340
📦 317 pieces 💵 $44.99 👤 6 minifigs ⏱ Ages 6+

What you get: Six Marvel minifigs with festive accessories — Spider-Man, Iron Man, Dr. Doom, Ghost Spider, Captain America, and Wolverine — plus 24 days of accessory builds. (Heads-up on Wolverine: at least one source reports he appears as a "Christmas tree built from Wolverine claws," so verify the lineup on the LEGO product page before you buy if Wolverine is the one you want.)

Six minifigs is the highest count in the 2026 lineup and the chase here is Dr. Doom and Wolverine. Both characters anchor major 2026 Marvel sets and the holiday variants are typically $8–$15 singles on BrickLink by February. If you want a calendar that is a portfolio rather than a diorama, Marvel is the smart buy.

Verdict: The chase. Six minifigs including two major holdovers from this year's Marvel wave. Best value if you only care about the figures.
3

LEGO City Advent Calendar — Santa's Workshop

Set 60510
📦 252 pieces 👤 Santa, Mrs. Claus, elves 🎄 Fold-out play mat ⏱ Ages 5+

What you get: A Santa minifigure, Mrs. Claus, elves, and reindeer, plus a fold-out Santa's Workshop play mat that turns the empty calendar into a tabletop scene. It is the only 2026 calendar built around traditional Christmas iconography — the Star Wars and Marvel options are IP-driven, the City one is a Christmas one.

Here is what matters for collectors: LEGO's Santa minifigures retire every year. The 2024 version is different from the 2023 version is different from the 2022 version. Keeping a Santa off every year's City calendar is one of the simplest LEGO collecting projects there is, and this year adds Mrs. Claus to the mix.

Verdict: The family pick. Best choice if the calendar is for a 5–10 year old or if you collect retiring Santa figures. The play mat is a genuinely clever touch.
4

LEGO Disney Princess Advent Calendar

Set 43298
📦 290 pieces 👤 4 minidolls ⏱ Ages 5+

What you get: Four Disney Princess minidolls — Ariel, Mulan, Cinderella, and Moana — plus animal companions and themed micro-builds for each. New for 2026: four Princesses across four different films makes this the most variety any single Disney calendar has delivered.

Minidolls, as a reseller note, do not hold value the way minifigures do — the secondary market for minidolls is thinner and the price floor is lower. But for a Princess fan under 10, this is the clear buy.

Verdict: The right gift for the right kid. Four Princesses is a strong year.
5

LEGO Friends Advent Calendar

Set 42698
💵 $27–$35 👤 Mini dolls + mini animals ⏱ Ages 6+

What you get: A winter-themed vignette of Friends minidolls and small animals with cold-weather accessories — sleds, mittens, hot cocoa, scarves. The price floor is the lowest of the five calendars, which is also the saving grace.

Friends calendars have historically been the lightest-weight in the lineup. 2026 is no exception. The upside: it is the cheapest, and for a Friends fan it still delivers 24 days of surprises.

Verdict: The budget pick. Grab it for a dedicated Friends fan, skip it otherwise.

The reseller angle: what will flood BrickLink in January

Here is a perspective you will not get from LEGO.com. Every January, parted-out advent calendars send a wave of loose holiday-variant minifigs into the BrickLink market. For 2026, here is what we expect to see:

  • Holiday Mando, holiday Moff Gideon, holiday IG-11 — these three will be the chase. Star Wars calendar sales always lead to a two-week minifig flood, then a slow burn as collectors absorb them. Buy by March if you want retail-equivalent pricing.
  • Holiday Dr. Doom, holiday Wolverine — the Marvel chase pair. Dr. Doom is a 2026 star across multiple sets and this holiday variant will be the one collectors want.
  • This year's Santa + Mrs. Claus — retired by this time next year, which is when prices start climbing.
  • Advent-only Pit Droid, Grogu variants — the small figures often get overlooked but historically they are the ones that disappear from the market fastest.

If you are hunting a specific calendar figure after the holidays, search our Product Finder or browse the BrickLink store — we restock advent minifigs through January and February.

How to buy at retail

Four quick notes:

  1. Star Wars sells out first. Every year, without fail. LEGO.com is usually the first to go out of stock, Amazon and Target follow a week later. If you want retail, grab it now.
  2. Amazon is the deal watch. Historically Amazon runs sub-retail pricing on these calendars in early November, and gizmodo flagged this already happening in 2026 for all three top calendars. Set a price alert.
  3. Target and Walmart carry them too, but inventory is smaller and the deals are usually not as deep as Amazon.
  4. Don't open before December 1. It is a rule for a reason. The slow-reveal is half the point — and the figures accumulate into a complete scene.

The bottom line

Five calendars this year. One obvious winner (Star Wars), one sleeper for collectors (Marvel), one family default (City), and two right-fit-only picks (Disney and Friends). The smaller lineup means every calendar gets more attention this year, so inventory will move faster than it has in a while. If you are planning to buy, do it in the next three weeks.
