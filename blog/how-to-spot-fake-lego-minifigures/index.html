How to Spot a Fake LEGO Minifigure: 5 Checks Before You Buy

Piece Pavilion · November 1, 2026 · 4 min read

The fastest way to spot a fake LEGO minifigure is to look at the studs, not the face. Genuine LEGO elements carry the LEGO logo molded onto the top of every stud, and most knockoff figures skip it or get it wrong. Here is the checklist we run on a figure before it goes in a bag at Piece Pavilion.

Check the studs and the torso first

Flip the figure's head, hands and hips over and look at the stud tops under good light. On genuine parts the LEGO logo is crisp and centered. On clones it is missing, blurry, misspelled or off-center. The same goes for the stud on top of the head and the studs on accessories like hair and helmets.

Then look at the torso print. LEGO's printing is sharp at the edges, with colors that stay inside their lines. Fakes tend to show fuzzy outlines, color that bleeds, or a print that sits slightly crooked on the torso. If the arms and torso do not match in shade, treat that as a warning sign rather than a quirk.

Feel the plastic and test the fit

LEGO uses ABS plastic with a slightly glossy, smooth finish. Knockoff plastic often feels brittle, waxy or too shiny, and parts may smell strongly of chemicals. Seams and flash (thin ridges of extra plastic) are more visible on clones.

Fit is the second tell. Genuine legs, torsos and heads click together firmly and come apart without a fight. Clone parts are often loose, so heads spin freely or hips will not hold a pose, or they are so tight they scuff or crack when you pull them apart. Test a figure against a part you know is real.

Know which figures get faked the most

Counterfeiters go after the figures with the highest resale value: Star Wars characters, Marvel and DC heroes, and collectible minifigure series figures. If a rare figure is priced far below what comparable listings are asking, ask why. A price that looks too good is the most common reason a buyer ends up with a clone. Our guide to the most valuable LEGO minifigures shows which figures deserve extra scrutiny.

Watch for these listing red flags

  • Stock photos only, with no pictures of the actual figure.
  • No close-ups of the studs, torso back or leg print.
  • A seller with a handful of reviews selling a long list of rare figures.
  • Descriptions like "LEGO compatible" or "custom" buried in the text.
  • Whole sets of figures sold as a bundle from unbranded sellers.

Buying from an established store with feedback you can read is still the simplest protection. You can check our feedback on the Piece Pavilion BrickLink store.

What to do if you already own one

A clone is not worthless, since it can still be a fine display or play piece. Just do not sell it as genuine, and keep it away from your real collection so it does not get mixed back in. If you are unsure about a figure, send us photos of the studs and torso through the contact page and we will tell you what we see.

Bottom line

Check the studs, check the print, check the fit. If any one fails, assume the figure is not genuine and walk away or ask for more photos.
