34 LEGO Video Game Sets Are Retiring This Year: Which Ones Are Worth Buying Now

Piece Pavilion · November 13, 2026 · 4 min read

Thirty-four. That's how many LEGO video game sets are headed for retirement by the end of this year, according to Video Games Chronicle and Nintendo Wire. Super Mario, Animal Crossing, Sonic, Horizon. If you've been telling yourself you'll grab one "eventually," eventually has about seven weeks left.

Why the video game wave matters more than the Star Wars one

Every year the retiring lists get dominated by Star Wars and City, and every year the Star Wars crowd gets the attention. Video game sets are a different animal. The buyer isn't only a LEGO collector. It's a Nintendo or PlayStation fan who bought one set for a shelf, and that crowd doesn't watch retirement trackers.

That's the gap. When a set's audience doesn't track LEGO news, the retirement notice reaches them late, if at all. Then it's gone from lego.com and the only place left is the secondary market.

The list is bigger than the headline

Brick Fanatics says more than 300 LEGO sets are retiring before the end of the year. Brickfact's tracker has swung between roughly 280 and roughly 450 depending on when you look, and most of its expected dates say December 2026. Treat every one of those numbers as an estimate. LEGO doesn't publish exact retirement dates, and sets get pulled early or quietly extended.

So 34 video game sets is a slice of a very large pile. Not a tiny slice, though. If you collect one theme, this is one of the bigger single-theme exits in a while.

Dates are guesses. Plan like it

Brick Fanatics has argued that some year-end sets could get pushed back, and it named the 40955 Maersk Dual-Fuel Container Vessel as one candidate. That's speculation, and it cuts both ways. A set you assume has until January might be gone in December. A set you rush-buy might still be on shelves in March.

Here's how I'd think about it as someone who handles used and retired LEGO all day:

  • Buy the one you'd actually build. If you'd be disappointed not having it in six months, don't gamble on an extension.
  • Skip the ones you're buying "as an investment." Nobody can promise you a retired set's price, and anyone who does is selling something.
  • Check the sets in multiple regions. Brick Fanatics notes the 75192 Millennium Falcon is sold out in some regions while stock remains in others. Same logic applies here.

What I'd do with a budget of about three sets

Pick the one you'd build this weekend, the one a kid in your house will actually play with, and one wildcard from a franchise you love. Buy those before Black Friday week, when the retiring stuff tends to get picked over first.

And if you miss one? Used sets exist for exactly this reason. A second-hand set from a clean seller, with the minifigs and a parts check, is a perfectly fine way to get a retired build later. It just won't come with the box discount.

The takeaway

The retirement list is long and the dates are fuzzy. Don't chase all 34. Pick the ones you'd miss and buy them while they're still on the shelf.
