LEGO Minifigure Stocking Stuffers: How to Pick One Worth Giving

Piece Pavilion · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

A single minifigure is the easiest LEGO gift to get right. It fits in a stocking, it does not need a building plan, and the person opening it knows what it is the second they see it. The catch is that buying one well takes a little more care than grabbing a set off a shelf.

Pick the character, not the theme

Sets are bought by theme. Minifigures are loved by character. A kid who builds Ninjago sets may care about one ninja in particular, and an adult collector may only want one Star Wars figure from a set they will never buy. Ask what the person already likes, or look at what is on their shelf, and buy that one figure rather than a random pack.

Know what you are paying for

A single figure usually costs far less than the set it came in, because you skip the bricks you do not need. Rare or discontinued figures are the exception, since the minifigure can be worth more than the rest of the set. Before you buy, search the figure on BrickLink and compare a few listings. If one seller is far above the rest, it is a pricing gap, not a rarity premium.

Check the figure is complete

A figure is more than a torso. Most come with a head, torso, legs, headgear or hair, and one or more accessories. Accessories go missing first, so confirm in the listing that the weapon, tool or hat is included. Ask the seller to list what comes with it, and compare against the BrickLink inventory for the set number.

  • Printing. Look for faded or scratched torso and face prints. Photos should be clear enough to see them.
  • Genuine LEGO. Fakes exist, especially for popular characters. Our guide on spotting fake minifigures covers what to check.
  • Condition. Used is fine. Cracked legs and chewed hair are not.

Build a gift around two or three

One figure is a stocking stuffer. Two or three from the same theme make a small scene: a hero, a villain and a sidekick. Group them in a small bag or box with a note about who they are. A gift like that feels chosen, and it usually costs less than a mid-sized set.

Order early, and keep it flexible

Holiday shipping slows down and popular figures sell out. Order a few weeks ahead, and keep a backup character in mind in case your first pick goes. If you are buying from our store, our reserve guide explains how to hold items.

The takeaway

Buy the character the person actually likes, confirm the figure is complete and genuine, and group two or three together. That beats a random set almost every time.
