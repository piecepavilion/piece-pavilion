BrickLink SOS Part 7: Three Years Apart, the Same November Questions

Piece Pavilion · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

BrickLink is down again, and the dates caught our attention. The 2023 shutdown started on November 3. This time, reports place the start late on November 5 or into November 6, 2026. Three years later, we're back in almost the same part of November, waiting for the marketplace to return.

For sellers like us at Piece Pavilion, it's easy to see why people are asking questions. When your shop depends on BrickLink, a long shutdown affects much more than a few minutes of browsing. Buyers can't place their usual orders, sellers can't trade normally, and everyone is trying to work out what comes next.

So we went back through the records. What happened in 2023? Where does 2022 fit in? And how much does any of it really tell us about today?

Before we go further: this is a comparison and an unconfirmed theory. BrickLink has not confirmed that the 2026 outage was caused by a hack, and we have no established link between these incidents. The similar dates are real. A shared cause is still unproven.

Why the November dates stand out

November 3, 2023 was a Friday. In 2026, November 5 was a Thursday and November 6 was a Friday. Both interruptions landed near a weekend in early November, just two or three calendar days apart when you compare the dates.

That's close enough to make us look twice. But we shouldn't call it the exact same day or hour. We don't have a verified start time for this outage, and an overnight shutdown can show up under different dates depending on the reporter's time zone.

Here's the short version of the timeline:

  • November 3–8, 2023: BrickLink took the marketplace offline after a threat and ransom demand. It returned roughly five days later.
  • Late November 5–6, 2026: Reports place the start of the current outage here.
  • November 9, 2026: The estimated return moved to November 11.
  • November 11, 2026: BrickLink said it had identified “unusual activity.” The reopening date was removed.
  • Evening of November 11, 2026 (EST): When we checked for this article, there was still no reopening time. This closure had already lasted longer than the roughly five-day shutdown in 2023.

The later estimates said 18:00 CET: noon EST, 9:00 a.m. PST, or 17:00 UTC. They also said “until at least,” so those were earliest estimates rather than promises. Brick Fanatics recorded those changes. Reports disagree about the hour given in the first Friday estimate, so we're leaving that hour out rather than guessing.

2023: a ransom demand, compromised accounts and five days offline

There's more to the 2023 story than the shorthand “BrickLink was hacked.” BrickLink said it had been investigating suspicious discount-selling activity since mid-October. Then, on November 3, a threat and ransom demand led it to take the site offline.

According to a November 8 notice LEGO BrickLink filed with Maryland's Attorney General, the demand was €50,000, about US$53,600 at the time. It came with threats of deletion and disruption. BrickLink said it did not pay and reported the incident to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center, known as IC3.

BrickLink believed the attackers had used usernames and passwords obtained elsewhere to get into accounts. That's called credential stuffing: trying stolen login details on another service, hoping someone has reused them.

Its reopening statement also said there was “no evidence so far that our systems were compromised.” That wording is important. Someone getting into a seller's account doesn't, by itself, prove they broke into the systems running the whole marketplace.

The Maryland filing listed five affected Maryland residents. That wasn't a worldwide total. It also described password resets and an additional two-factor process for directly affected accounts. Sellers were advised to check their inventories after reopening.

By November 8, BrickLink was online again. Jay's Brick Blog reported seeing it back by 1:20 p.m. EST, or 18:20 UTC, and reproduced BrickLink's statement. That gives us a reported observation, rather than an exact official restart time.

This time, we still have a big missing piece

BrickLink's official November 11 recovery notice says: “We took BrickLink offline after identifying unusual activity on the platform.” It describes containing the activity, investigating it and checking for related issues before reopening.

Of course that raises security questions. But the notice doesn't identify an attacker, confirm a ransom, give an affected-account count or say whether customer data was exposed. It doesn't confirm a hack as the cause.

That's the biggest difference in this comparison. With 2023, we have a later explanation of what BrickLink believed had happened. With 2026, we're still waiting for that level of detail. The shutdowns look familiar from a seller's side, but we can't yet say the problems behind them are the same.

What about the stories from 2022?

Two separate events tend to come up here, and they deserve to be kept separate.

In March 2022, a cyber incident involving a partner affected certain cross-border transactions. That included non-EU sellers shipping to EU buyers and non-UK sellers shipping to UK buyers. A March 16 report carried BrickLink's statement that service had been restored and no BrickLink data had been compromised. It wasn't documented as a shutdown of the entire marketplace.

Later that year, security researchers at Salt Labs found vulnerabilities on October 18. They reported them to LEGO on October 23, received an acknowledgment on October 25, and tested again on November 10. By that retest, the attacks they had demonstrated no longer worked. Their report became public on December 15, 2022.

The original Salt Labs research describes ways to take over accounts and access server information. It does not establish that criminals used those methods. The researchers said they couldn't confirm or deny whether others had exploited them.

So, could the incidents be connected?

It's a fair question. Seeing another major shutdown in almost the same November window, three years later, is what sent us looking in the first place.

A repeat attack is one possibility people may wonder about. So are unrelated security problems and coincidence. Right now, we don't have published evidence tying the same attackers or methods to these events. We also don't have enough information to put an honest percentage on the connection.

For sellers, though, the effect needs very little explaining. A shop can have stock ready to go and customers ready to buy, yet still be stuck waiting. Another missed estimate makes planning harder. That's why clear updates matter so much, even when the update can't give us the answer we want.

We want BrickLink back, and we want the people working on it to get it back safely. The dates make this worth asking about. They don't give us the answer. For now, we're keeping the question open and the facts separate from the theory, with the same hope as everyone else: getting our shops moving again.
