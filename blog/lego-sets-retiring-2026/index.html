LEGO Sets Retiring in 2026: Buy Before They're Gone (and Why Your Old Sets Are Worth More)

Piece Pavilion · June 15, 2026

Here's a number worth paying attention to: more than 120 LEGO sets are scheduled to leave shelves for good on July 31, 2026 — including 18 hard-to-replace exclusives. Some are already gone. The $299.99 LEGO The Legend of Zelda Great Deku Tree (77092) just sold out on LEGO's own U.S. store ahead of its retirement, which means the only place left to find one is the secondhand market. If there's a set you've been eyeing — or one quietly sitting in a closet — the next few weeks matter.

What "retiring" actually means for the price

When LEGO retires a set, it stops making it. Existing stock sells through, and once it's gone, it's gone — there's no restock. Demand doesn't disappear, though, so prices on the secondhand market typically climb after retirement, sometimes well past the original retail price. (We break it down in our guide to where to buy retired LEGO sets.) That's the whole reason retired sets are some of the most sought-after items we carry.

Notable sets retiring July 31, 2026

A few highlights from this summer's end-of-life list:

  • Harry Potter: 76417 Gringotts Wizarding Bank – Collectors' Edition, 76437 The Burrow – Collectors' Edition, 76435 Hogwarts Castle: The Great Hall, and 76453 Malfoy Manor.
  • Star Wars: three Starship Collection ships — 75404 Acclamator-Class Assault Ship, 75356 Executor Super Star Destroyer, and 75405 Home One Starcruiser.
  • Icons & Ideas: 21348 Dungeons & Dragons: Red Dragon's Tale, 21350 Jaws, and 21352 The Magic of Disney.
  • The Legend of Zelda: 77092 Great Deku Tree 2-in-1 — already sold out in the U.S.

That's a small slice. The full list runs past 120 sets across City, Marvel, Technic, Architecture, and more — so whatever theme you collect, something you want is probably on it.

And a bigger wave at the end of 2026

Looking a little further out, the end-of-year list is stacked too. Every interactive LEGO Super Mario model is on the way out, along with the Fortnite 77077 Klombo, the lone LEGO Horizon set (77037 Aloy & Varl), and the Star Wars 75435 Separatist MTT. If any of those are on your wishlist, it's worth planning ahead rather than scrambling in December.

Why this is actually good news (if you shop smart)

Here's the part that gets missed: retirement doesn't make a set unavailable — it just moves it to the secondhand market. That's exactly where we live. Every set and minifigure we carry is hand-inspected, accurately described, and packed with care, so a retired set shows up looking its best instead of being a gamble. Browsing our shelves often beats paying scalper prices for a sealed box — and you can buy just the figures or parts you actually want. Start with our retired sets or the full store.

Sitting on retired sets? They're worth more than you think

The flip side is just as important: if you've got older or retired sets gathering dust, this is a good moment to look at them with fresh eyes. As sets retire and demand builds, the value of what you already own quietly goes up.
