BrickLink SOS Part 7: The Craziness Continues

Piece Pavilion · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

BrickLink is still down, and the latest update is not what many of us were hoping to hear.

After days of waiting for the marketplace to return, BrickLink says it took the site offline after identifying “unusual activity.” Its team is still investigating, and right now there is no date or time for when BrickLink will be back.

For sellers, buyers and builders who depend on BrickLink every day, that leaves a big question: What happens now?

Welcome to BrickLink SOS Part 7. The craziness continues.

The countdown has changed

BrickLink had previously said the site would be unavailable until at least noon Eastern on Wednesday, November 11. Many of us were watching that time closely, hoping it would mark the beginning of a return to normal.

Instead, BrickLink replaced the expected return time with a new explanation. In its official update, the company says it is working to secure the site, protect its members and investigate what happened before bringing the marketplace back online.

That means there is no new countdown to share. BrickLink could return sooner or later, but any specific date right now would be a guess.

The missing date makes planning harder. A buyer cannot know whether to wait another afternoon or rethink a time-sensitive project. A seller cannot confidently promise when an unanswered question or unfinished order can be checked. Even a rough estimate can help people organize their work, but it only helps when it is reliable.

Was BrickLink hacked?

This is the question many people are asking, and it’s understandable why. Words like “unusual activity,” “secure the site” and “containing the issue” sound like a possible security problem.

A hack is one possibility. But BrickLink has not confirmed a successful hack or said that customer information was stolen. It has not identified the cause, explained exactly what happened or said which accounts, if any, were affected.

We want to be very clear: a possible security incident is not the same thing as a confirmed data breach. We don’t want to tell you everything is fine before the investigation is finished, and we also don’t want to present rumors as facts.

The length of the outage cannot tell us exactly what happened, either. A platform may need time to investigate suspicious activity and check its systems before reopening. That is a general reason an investigation can take time, not inside information about BrickLink. We should be careful about turning the number of days offline into proof of a particular attack.

As more information becomes available, we’ll look to BrickLink’s own updates and reliable reporting for answers.

Almost a week without BrickLink

BrickLink went offline late last week, and by November 11 the interruption is approaching a full week. What started as a temporary interruption has become a much longer wait for a community that relies on this marketplace. With no return date now available, sellers and buyers have to plan without knowing when they’ll be able to use it again.

For a casual visitor, that might mean waiting to buy a few pieces. For a small business, it can mean lost sales, customer questions and an entire routine placed on hold.

BrickLink is more than a website where people buy LEGO pieces. For many sellers, it is where they manage inventory, check orders, communicate with customers and run their stores. When the site is unavailable, several parts of the business can be affected at once.

How sellers are feeling the impact

Imagine receiving an order before the outage and then being unable to open the order page to check an address, a buyer’s message or a detail about the items. Even when a seller wants to help, the information needed to do so may be unavailable.

There can also be a financial impact. In Brick Fanatics’ interviews with sellers, one seller reported that his overall sales revenue fell 52% compared with the same four-day period the previous November, even though his sales on another marketplace increased.

That is one seller’s experience, not a number for every BrickLink store. But it shows how serious a prolonged outage can become for a small business.

Some missed purchases may come back when the site reopens. Others may not, especially when a buyer needs pieces for a gift or a deadline. We cannot put a reliable total on those losses, but sellers have good reason to care about every additional day.

At Piece Pavilion, we understand how much time and care goes into sorting pieces, maintaining inventory, packing orders and helping customers. When a platform that supports all of that work goes offline, the effects can reach well beyond a single missed sale.

Buyers and builders are affected too

Buyers may be wondering about orders they already placed. Builders may be waiting for the last few pieces needed to finish a project. Collectors may be unable to search for a particular minifigure or compare prices.

Some websites outside BrickLink also depend on its information. Brickset reported that the outage affected images and BrickLink-supplied price and availability information on its own site. That shows how far the interruption can reach.

If you have an order in progress, remember that an inaccessible order page does not automatically mean something went wrong. A package might already be on its way, and a seller might be having the same trouble accessing BrickLink that you are.

What can you do while waiting?

There may not be a way to make BrickLink reopen faster, but there are a few practical things buyers and sellers can do:

  • Keep order confirmations, payment receipts and tracking numbers together.
  • If you have a tracking number, check it directly with the shipping carrier.
  • Sellers can write down any inventory changes or orders handled elsewhere so they can compare them with BrickLink later.
  • Avoid placing a duplicate order or sending a second payment just because you can’t currently see the first one.
  • Be careful with unexpected messages asking you to click a recovery link or make an urgent payment. Go to official websites directly when checking for updates.

These are sensible precautions during an interruption. They are not a claim that any particular account or order has been affected.

What would help the community now?

Members need to know whether there is anything they should do to protect or recover their accounts, what guidance applies to interrupted orders, and when another update will arrive. BrickLink does not need to share sensitive technical details to make those next steps clearer.

A regular update, even without a reopening date, would give sellers and buyers something dependable to check instead of refreshing the same page and sorting through rumors.

We’re hoping for answers, and a safe return

It’s frustrating to lose the expected return time, especially after waiting so long. Sellers need to plan, buyers want updates, and the entire LEGO community wants to know what comes next.

At the same time, we want BrickLink’s team to have the time it needs to investigate and bring the site back safely. A quick return would be wonderful, but a secure return with clear instructions matters too.

For now, the facts are simple: BrickLink is offline, unusual activity is being investigated, and there is no confirmed reopening date. We’ll keep following the official updates and sharing what we learn.

To everyone at BrickLink working on the problem, thank you. To the buyers, sellers and builders waiting alongside us: we’re all hoping for good news soon.

Information checked November 11, 2026. Details may change as BrickLink’s investigation continues.
