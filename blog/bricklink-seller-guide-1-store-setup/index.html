BrickLink Guide #1: Setting Your Store Up for Volume

Piece Pavilion · November 3, 2026 · 5 min read

A few times a week we post a short guide for other BrickLink sellers — the small, specific things that have actually helped us move inventory. New guides land every Monday, Wednesday and Friday. If one of them helps you, that is a win.

This first guide is the one we think about most: setting your store up so inventory moves without you babysitting it.

1. Tag every lot with a real bin location, in Remarks

The biggest difference between a store that packs orders in ten minutes and a store that packs them in forty is whether every lot has a bin location in its Remarks field. BrickLink prints Remarks on the pick sheet, and your eyes stop looking at the part number as soon as you see something like A3-R or B12-top. Pick a scheme that fits your space (aisle-shelf-position works well) and stick to it. If a lot has no bin, it does not exist on your pick sheet.

We tag every single lot in Remarks before it goes live. Rename a bin and you only have to touch the lots in that one bin, not every lot in the store.

2. Write Description once, reuse it as a macro

The Description field is where most sellers either over-write (a paragraph on every lot) or under-write (nothing at all). Both lose you time. Pick five to ten reusable Description blocks for the categories you sell most — one for sealed minifigs, one for used technic parts, one for bulk lot bags, and so on — and paste the right one in when you list. Buyers read the first line and skim the rest, so lead with the condition and any flaw.

A good Description is specific enough that a buyer never writes to ask "is this really new" or "does it have the sticker." Every message you prevent is five minutes you got back.

3. Photos that reduce questions, not photos that look pretty

You do not need studio lighting. You need a photo that shows the exact thing a buyer is uncertain about. For most used parts that is one top-down shot in good natural light on a plain white background. For minifigs that is a clear torso-front picture and a back-of-torso picture, because the two most common buyer questions are "is the print worn" and "is this the version with the back print."

If you cannot answer the common question with a photo, the lot is going to generate messages until it sells. Spend thirty seconds on the picture, save hours over the lot's life.

4. Write your Store Terms so they handle the five awkward situations

Most buyers never read Store Terms. The ones who read them do it after something awkward happens, and that is when your terms earn their keep. Yours should answer, in two or three lines each, these five situations:

  • Minimum order and combined shipping on repeat orders in a short window.
  • What happens if a lot is miscounted or missing on your end — refund, replace or credit.
  • How you handle a buyer-requested cancellation before shipping.
  • Non-paying buyer policy and the window before you cancel.
  • International shipping rules, including the countries you do not ship to.

Write once. Reference it every time a buyer asks. If a situation keeps coming up that is not in Terms, add it — your Terms should grow with your store.

5. Anchor your pricing to the six-month average, not the peak

We re-price inventory weekly against the BrickLink price guide's last six-month new-condition average, not the all-time peak. A figure that printed $17 in September can trade $14 in November, and the sellers who leave the tag at $17 just stop moving inventory. The six-month average is close enough to today's reality that your prices stay competitive, and far enough back that you are not chasing weekly noise.

If you sell a lot of used parts, the same rule applies with the used-condition six-month average. If you only have a handful of listings in a given category, pick the ten most expensive and re-price those; the long tail of cheap bricks almost never moves the needle.
