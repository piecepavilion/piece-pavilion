LEGO's $800 Sagrada Família Is the Cheapest Set of November Per Piece

Piece Pavilion · November 18, 2026 · 4 min read

The most expensive LEGO set that launched this month is also the cheapest per piece, by a wide margin. 21065 Sagrada Família is 12,060 pieces for $799.99. That's about 6.6 cents a brick. The $22.99 Cosy Plants set works out to 9.3.

November's whole lineup, in cents per piece

It was a quiet release month, just four or five sets depending on how you count, all dated November 1. Here's the math on each:

  • 21065 Sagrada Família: 12,060 pieces, $799.99, about 6.6 cents per piece.
  • 11388 Venetian Neighbourhood: 5,682 pieces, $549.99, about 9.7 cents per piece. It comes with a 322-piece gift-with-purchase, 40898 Venetian Water Taxi, which I'm not counting in that number.
  • 11390 Dragon Ball: Shenron & Goku: 1,764 pieces, $179.99, about 10.2 cents per piece.
  • 10371 Cosy Plants: 248 pieces, $22.99, about 9.3 cents per piece.

Sagrada Família beats the next-cheapest set by roughly a third. The Dragon Ball set, the licensed one, is the most expensive per brick.

Why the big one wins on paper

Price per piece usually falls as sets get bigger, and a 12,000-piece box is about as big as LEGO sells. A licensed theme adds a cost on top, which fits Dragon Ball landing at the top of the list. That's my read of the pattern, not something LEGO has said.

The Venetian set is the odd one. It's less than half the size of Sagrada Família and costs 9.7 cents a piece. That's a premium for a build most people will treat as a display piece.

The Venetian math is stranger than it looks

Venetian Neighbourhood costs $549.99 for 5,682 pieces. Sagrada Família costs $250 more and gives you 6,378 additional pieces. Do that division and the extra pieces cost about 3.9 cents each. In other words, the jump from the second-biggest set to the biggest is nearly free by LEGO standards.

That doesn't make it a bargain for everyone. It's still $800, and it's a lot of build. But if you were already about to spend $550 on the Venetian set, you should at least look at what the extra $250 buys.

Where the yardstick breaks

Per-piece price treats every part as equal. A 1x1 plate and a big curved panel count the same. I haven't seen the part lists for these sets, so I can't tell you how much of Sagrada Família is small detail pieces. If it's heavy on tiny parts, 6.6 cents buys less than it looks like.

Cosy Plants is the cheap way in. At $22.99 it's the only one here a person buys on a whim. Whether 9.3 cents a piece is good for a botanical set depends on what's in the bag, and I'd want to see the inventory first.

One more caveat: these numbers are list prices. Retail promos, gift-with-purchase offers and Black Friday deals shift the math fast, and the LEGO Group's Black Friday preview livestream was set for November 3, so deals are coming. Recheck the numbers when a promo hits.

What I'd actually do

If you've got $800 and shelf space for a monster, the math supports Sagrada Família. If you're watching the budget, the number to ignore is the headline price. Nobody picks a set because it has the best cents-per-piece. They pick it because they want to build it.

Pick the set you'd still want built in January. Then check the math.
