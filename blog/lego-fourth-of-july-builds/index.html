Red, White & Brick: 5 LEGO Builds for the Fourth of July

Piece Pavilion · June 29, 2026

The Fourth of July weekend is here, and it's the perfect excuse to break out the red, white, and blue bricks. Whether you're keeping the kids busy before the fireworks or you just want a festive piece for the shelf, here are five builds you can knock out this weekend — plus the parts that make each one work. (Handy timing: our 10% off used parts sale runs through tomorrow, June 30 — a good moment to stock up on colors.)

1. A brick-built American flag

The classic. Lay a base of red and white 1x2 and 1x4 plates for the stripes, add a blue rectangle in the corner, and dot it with white 1x1 round plates or tiles for stars. Build it flat as a mosaic to hang, or tile the surface for a smooth finished look. It's beginner-friendly and looks great on a desk or shelf.

2. Fireworks in mid-burst

Capture a firework frozen at the moment it bursts. Start from a central point and radiate outward with transparent and brightly colored pieces — bars, antennas, small cones, and round plates work great as sparks. Mount it on a black baseplate so the colors pop. Want the shortcut version? Sets like the Celebration Ferris Wheel with Fireworks bring the spark to a finished display.

3. A liftoff rocket

Nothing says celebration like a rocket. A white cylinder body, a red nose cone, blue fins, and a wash of trans-orange and trans-yellow at the base for flames — set it on a little launch gantry and you've got a centerpiece. Great for builders who want something with a bit of height and drama.

4. A backyard cookout scene

Pull a few minifigures and stage the all-American cookout: a grill (a few black and silver parts), a picnic table, a cooler, maybe a dog begging for a hot dog. Vignettes like this are where loose minifigs and accessories from a parts bin really shine — and it's a fun one to do with kids.

5. A parade float

Put wheels under a small platform, drape it in red-white-and-blue plates and flags, and load it with waving minifigures. Add a brick-built banner and you've got a rolling tribute. Bonus points for a tiny marching band.

Where to get the colors

The beauty of these builds is they run on common parts — red, white, and blue plates and tiles, a few trans pieces, and some minifigures. That's exactly what a parts store is for: grab the specific colors and counts you need instead of buying a whole set. Our product finder makes a specific brick easy to track down, and with used parts 10% off through June 30, this weekend's project just got cheaper.

However you celebrate, have a safe and happy Fourth — and happy building. 🎆
