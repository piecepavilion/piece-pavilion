LEGO Sets Retiring by December 31, 2026: What to Buy Now and What Might Get a Reprieve

Piece Pavilion · October 29, 2026 · 4 min read

More than 300 LEGO sets are scheduled to retire before the end of 2026, according to Brick Fanatics' running tracker, and Stonewars puts the final count closer to 400 once LEGO finishes updating its retiring list. If you have been telling yourself you will buy it later, later has a date on it.

We covered the July wave in LEGO Sets Retiring in 2026. This is the year-end sequel: what is on the list, what might get a reprieve, and how we would shop it.

The list is big, and it is still moving

Retirement lists are not fixed. Brick Fanatics reports that three LEGO Ideas sets have had six months taken off their time on shelves, while another has been given six months more. The same outlet also notes that a set can vanish earlier than its listed date if LEGO runs out of stock. A retirement date is the latest a set will be on sale, not a guarantee it will be there that long.

Our take: treat any set you actually want as already on the clock, whatever month it says.

Two year-end names worth knowing

  • 21323 Grand Piano is now listed to retire at the end of December 2026. It is a Ideas-line display piece, and exactly the sort of set that tends to get harder to find after it is gone.
  • 41843 Family Christmas Tree is also listed for the end of December, though no exact date has been given. A Christmas set that retires in December is a funny one: you have roughly one holiday season left to get it new.

These are two examples from a list of several hundred. Check the full tracker for your theme before you decide anything.

Sets that might slip into 2027

Brick Fanatics flags a handful of sets with a good chance of being pushed back, including 40955 Maersk Dual-Fuel Container Vessel and 10326 Natural History Museum. The same piece is careful to say there is also a good chance they retire on schedule after all.

That is the trap. Waiting to see whether a set gets extended is a bet, and the downside is paying secondhand prices for something you could have bought new. If the set is a must-have for you, do not make that bet. If you are indifferent, waiting costs you nothing.

How to shop it

  • Buy the one you would regret missing first. Do not split your budget across ten maybes.
  • Watch Black Friday. Retiring sets are where retailers tend to cut deepest, while flagship sets rarely drop far. We have not seen confirmed 2026 deals yet, so check back once they are announced.
  • Remember secondhand is an option. Once a set leaves LEGO's shelves, the aftermarket is the only place left. Our guide to where to buy retired LEGO sets covers how to do that without overpaying.

The takeaway

With 300-plus sets leaving by December 31 and dates that can move in either direction, the safest plan is simple: decide which one or two sets you truly want, and buy them while they are still on LEGO's shelves.
