Name That LEGO Piece: 71 Part Names Every Builder Should Know

Piece Pavilion · September 22, 2026

Every LEGO builder has done it. You hold up a piece, you know exactly what it does, and you have no idea what to call it. So you type “the little wedge thing” into a search box and get nothing. Or you ask a friend for a cheese slope and they hand you a Brick, Modified 1 x 1 with Headlight, because that is what their friend called it.

Names matter more than they used to. Buying single pieces on BrickLink, sorting a bulk lot, following a fan build online: all of it runs on part names and numbers. So we pulled the 71 pieces people ask us about most, put the nickname next to the official BrickLink name, and added the one thing you actually need: the part number that makes every search work.

Read it as a cheat sheet, then scroll to the bottom and take the quiz. Ten pieces, four names each. Post your score and tag @piecepavilion; we want to see who can beat the shop.

How to read a LEGO part name

Official names look like gibberish until you see the pattern. The BrickLink catalog, which the whole secondhand market runs on, names every piece the same way:

  1. The family first. Brick, Plate, Tile, Slope, Bracket, Technic, Minifigure. A comma after it means a sub-family: Plate, Modified or Slope, Curved.
  2. Then the size in studs. Width by length. When the part is not a whole brick tall, a third number gives the height in bricks, so x 2/3 is two plates tall and x 1/3 is one plate.
  3. Then what makes it special. “with Stud on Side,” “with Clip Vertical,” “Inverted,” “with Axle Hole.”
  4. The part number is molded on the piece. Look inside a brick or under a plate for tiny raised digits. A letter after the number (3070b, 4085d) marks a mold revision, and BrickLink sometimes drops it when the old version is retired.

Once you can read one name, you can read all of them. Every card below gives you the nickname, the official name, the number, and a live link to check whether we have it in stock.

Brick, plate, tile: the height rule

Three words cover most of a bulk bin. A brick is the unit of height. A plate is one-third of a brick. A tile is a plate with no stud on top. Dimensions are always studs wide by studs long, and when a part is not a whole brick tall the name adds a height, so 1 x 1 x 2/3 means one stud by one stud, two plates tall.

Technic: pins, axles and the color code

Still can’t identify a piece? Four ways to look it up

  1. Read the number off the part. Tilt it under a lamp. Most pieces made since the 1980s carry a four- or five-digit mold number inside or underneath. Type it into our Product Finder or the BrickLink catalog search.
  2. Photograph it. Free part-recognition tools such as Brickognize identify a piece from a phone photo with surprising accuracy and hand you the BrickLink number.
  3. Browse by shape. The BrickLink catalog is organized by the same families as this guide. If you know it is a slope, open Slope and scan the pictures.
  4. Ask us. Send a photo through our contact form or Instagram DMs. Identifying odd pieces is half our job, and we like doing it.

Frequently asked questions

What is a LEGO cheese slope?

A cheese slope is the nickname for LEGO part 54200, officially Slope 30 1 x 1 x 2/3. It is a one-stud wedge, two plates tall, with a 30-degree face, and it looks like a small wedge of cheese. The two-stud-wide version (85984) is often called a double cheese.

What is a jumper plate in LEGO?

A jumper plate is a 1 x 2 plate with a single stud in the center, part 15573 (older version 3794). It lets builders shift a piece half a stud off the normal grid, which is why it is called a jumper.

What is a LEGO headlight brick or Erling brick?

The headlight brick, part 4070, is officially Brick, Modified 1 x 1 with Headlight. It has a recessed stud on one side and was designed in 1979 by LEGO designer Erling Dideriksen to hold a transparent round plate as a car headlight, which is where both nicknames come from.

What is the difference between a LEGO brick, plate and tile?

A brick is the standard unit of height. A plate is one-third the height of a brick, so three plates stacked equal one brick. A tile is a plate with a smooth top and no stud. Part names give the footprint in studs first, so a 1 x 2 plate is one stud wide by two studs long.

What does SNOT mean in LEGO building?

SNOT stands for Studs Not On Top. It describes any technique that points studs sideways or downward, using parts like the 1 x 1 brick with a stud on the side (87087), the headlight brick (4070), brackets (99780 and 99781) and the Travis brick (4733).

How do I find out what a LEGO piece is called?

Look for the tiny part number molded on the inside or underside of the piece, then search that number in the BrickLink catalog or in the Piece Pavilion Product Finder. If you cannot read a number, photograph the piece with a part-recognition app such as Brickognize, or browse the BrickLink catalog by category (Brick, Plate, Slope, Tile) and shape.
