LEGO Ideas Godzilla (21375): The 5,363-Piece Set That Lands on Black Friday, and What It Means for the Resale Market

Piece Pavilion · November 4, 2026 · 5 min read

LEGO Ideas 21375 Godzilla goes on sale November 27, 2026 at 12 a.m. EST, direct from LEGO. The set is 5,363 pieces, retails for $449.99, stands over 27.5 inches (70 cm) tall and is rated 18+. The fan design behind it came from LEGO Ideas contributor Matthew Esposito and reached the 10,000-supporter threshold earlier in the year. This is one of the largest LEGO Ideas sets ever released, and it is going to move a lot of secondary-market traffic the moment it hits the shelves.

What is actually in the box

LEGO's own listing describes a brick-built Godzilla with scaly skin, jagged dorsal fins, and a posable tail, head, arms and fingers. The two headline play features are the attachable tongue and the Heat Ray effect piece, and the figure is built for display rather than for playing in the traditional LEGO sense — the official product page calls it a collectible meant for 18+ builders and “monster merch collectors.” The 5,363-piece count puts it in the same conversation as LEGO's largest display sets: the UCS Millennium Falcon (7,541), the Eiffel Tower (10,001) and the Titanic (9,090). The $449.99 price works out to about $0.084 per piece, which is roughly on-trend for an Ideas display set and cheaper per piece than the Falcon ($0.11) but more expensive than the Eiffel Tower ($0.063).

The Gift-With-Purchase window

LEGO is running a four-day GWP from 11/27 through 11/30 while supplies last: a brick-built Godzilla Logo and Footprint, free with purchase of the set. That GWP window is the entire ballgame for anyone buying to flip or buying to part out. Here is why.

  • GWP-included Godzilla sets become their own BrickLink item. Once the four-day window closes, the Logo and Footprint GWP will only come with sets purchased between 11/27 and 11/30. Secondary listings for sealed sets “with GWP” will trade at a premium over plain sealed sets within weeks. Sellers who bank several sealed sets with the GWP will carry an inventory that gradually appreciates against sealed-without-GWP listings.
  • The GWP itself becomes a listable BrickLink item. Every LEGO Ideas GWP in recent memory — the Tapestry of Winter from the Medieval Blacksmith, the First Order Stormtrooper Officer from the Death Star — has ended up with its own BrickLink listing within a quarter, then slowly climbed as GWP supply ran out. Expect the same arc here.
  • Don't open a sealed set to fish out the GWP. The sealed combined value is going to beat opened-plus-GWP for at least a year.

What this does to the parts-out market

A 5,363-piece set drops a large amount of inventory into BrickLink at once. The pattern on sets of this size over the last three years has been consistent: the first 90 days see heavy parts-out activity from aggressive sellers, prices on common parts in the set briefly dip because supply spikes, then unique molded parts rise and stay up while common parts drift back toward their pre-release baseline.

  • Unique molded parts are the trade. A brick-built Godzilla at this scale almost certainly introduces new molded elements for the dorsal fins, Heat Ray, tongue and claws. New molded parts appearing in only one set are the elements most worth stripping out and listing individually. Watch Rebrickable's inventory in the first week of December for which parts appear in no other set.
  • Dark Bluish Gray and Black commons will dip briefly. A 5,363-piece display set of a dark-colored monster is going to use a lot of dark common bricks. Expect a 10 to 15 percent dip in six-month averages on the highest-volume Dark Bluish Gray and Black plates and bricks through December, then a recovery by Q1 2027. That is a buying window for anyone replenishing core inventory, not a selling window.
  • The 18+ build audience doesn't part out at the same rate. Display collectors keep their sets built. That means parts-out supply for 21375 will be lower than, say, a 5,000-piece Star Wars battle set — which argues for holding, not immediately listing, the uncommon parts once the mold set is clear.

What to do before 11/27

A few things worth doing in the next three weeks, in order:

  1. Sign in to LEGO Insiders. Insiders buyers get first access on launch day and the GWP window will deplete within hours in that first wave. If you want sealed sets with the GWP for resale, you need to be in the queue the moment the clock turns.
  2. Set a BrickLink Wanted List alert for set number 21375. The first week of secondary listings is where the price discovery happens — a Wanted List alert puts you in front of mispriced listings before the arbitrage closes.
  3. Watch Rebrickable from early December for the full part inventory. The new-mold parts are the ones to list separately; the commons are the ones to leave in a parts-out pile and pick at over 2027.
  4. Decide your inventory position before buying. One sealed set to keep, one to sell sealed in six months, one to part out — or any other mix, but write it down before the GWP window closes. Impulse buys of 5,363-piece sets rarely end well.

Bottom line

Godzilla 21375 is a 5,363-piece, $449.99, 27.5-inch display set launching November 27 with a four-day Godzilla Logo and Footprint GWP. The sealed-with-GWP combination is the most interesting resale angle, the new-mold parts are where parts-out value will concentrate, and the first week of December will tell you which parts those are. If you plan to buy at retail, be ready at midnight EST on launch day.
