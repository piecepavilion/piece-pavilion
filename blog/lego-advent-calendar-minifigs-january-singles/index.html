LEGO Advent Calendar Minifigs in January: Buy the Box Now or the Singles Later?

Piece Pavilion · November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Every January, the same thing happens on BrickLink: holiday-variant minifigs that only came out of an Advent calendar show up in bulk, listed by people who opened 24 doors and had no use for the figures. If you're deciding between a calendar now and the loose figures later, the timing matters more than the sticker price.

Why the figures show up in January, not December

Nobody lists a calendar minifig on December 8. The calendar is still in play, the kid is still opening doors, and the buyer pool is full of people who just want the whole box. The listings land after the 25th, when the calendar is empty and the figures are the leftover.

That's the window. Supply spikes, and the single most-wanted figure from each calendar is usually the one that holds its price while the filler figures sink.

The Star Wars calendar is the one to watch

The 2026 Star Wars calendar (75456) is a Mandalorian box: 293 pieces, $44.99, five minifigs plus Grogu and a Pit Droid as small figures. Two of the five, the holiday-variant Moff Gideon and IG-11, don't exist in any other set. That's the whole story with exclusive variants. There's one source of supply, and it's whatever people open this month.

Our earlier ranking flagged this calendar as the one that tends to sell out at the LEGO store by mid-November. If that holds, the loose figures get scarcer in January for the simplest reason there is: fewer calendars were opened.

Marvel is the opposite bet

The Marvel calendar (76340) is 317 pieces, also $44.99, and carries six minifigs, the highest count in this year's lineup. Dr. Doom and Wolverine are the two to chase. In our earlier piece we pegged holiday-variant singles like these at roughly $8-$15 each on BrickLink by February.

Six figures is a lot of supply. Marvel calendars were less likely to sell out, so more of them get opened and more of those figures reach the market. Expect the January listings to be deeper, and prices on the non-chase figures to soften faster.

What a calendar figure is, and isn't

A calendar minifig is a seasonal exclusive, not a rarity in the way a convention figure is. LEGO makes a lot of these, and the supply is fixed by one thing: how many boxes got opened. That's why the pricing story differs from calendar to calendar. A box that sells out early produces fewer loose figures. A box that sits on shelves into December produces more.

So the useful question isn't "which figure is rare." It's "which calendar will be scarce by January." This year that looks like Star Wars. Don't treat that as a guarantee. It's a pattern from earlier years, and it can miss.

It also means the usual advice, grab whatever's cheapest, backfires here. The cheapest route to a specific figure depends on how many of that calendar get opened, which you can't see until it's too late to buy the box.

So buy the box or wait for the singles?

Here's how I'd split it.

  • You want the experience of 24 doors: buy the box now. Nothing in January replaces a kid opening one every morning.
  • You want one or two specific figures: wait. Singles are cheaper than a whole calendar if you only want a Moff Gideon or a Dr. Doom, and you skip the leftover pile entirely.
  • You want the exclusive Star Wars variants: don't wait past mid-November. The box may be gone, and the singles market will be thin.

One caution. I've only got price ranges from earlier this season, not live listings, so check current BrickLink sold prices before you commit to a number. Asking prices in January run higher than what figures actually sell for.

The takeaway

Buy the box if you want the ritual. Buy the singles in January if you want the figures. And if it's the Mandalorian ones you're after, buy this week.
