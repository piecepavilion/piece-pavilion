The LEGO Halloween Minifigure Guide: Every Spooky CMF, Plus Parts to Build Your Own

Piece Pavilion · October 15, 2026

Halloween is two weeks out, which means the trick-or-treat bowl is on the counter, the plastic skeleton is back on the porch, and the group chat is asking what to put on the shelf. For LEGO builders the answer is easy: LEGO has quietly produced more Halloween minifigures than almost any other seasonal theme. You just have to know where they're hiding.

Here is a field guide to the spooky LEGO minifigures that already exist, from the all-monsters series to the classics scattered across early Collectible Minifigures packs, plus the parts you need to custom-build the ghost, witch or jack-o'-lantern that LEGO never made. If you want to put a Halloween row on your shelf this year, this is where to start.

The crown jewel: Collectible Minifigures Series 14 (Monsters)

If you only chase one series for Halloween, make it this one. In 2015 LEGO released Collectible Minifigures Series 14 (set 71010), a full blind-bag series built entirely around monsters. Every figure in the box was Halloween-themed, which has never happened again before or since.

The 16 figures run the gamut of horror-movie staples — a Wacky Witch on a broom, a Zombie Businessman clutching his briefcase, a Skeleton Guy under a hoodie, a glowing Specter, a Plant Monster, a Gargoyle, a Monster Rocker, a Monster Scientist, a Fly Monster, a Spider Lady, a Banshee, a Square Foot (yes, bigfoot got in), plus a Zombie Pirate, a Zombie Cheerleader and a Tiger Woman for jungle-horror variety. Many came with glow-in-the-dark or trans-neon accessories, which was unusual at the time and part of the reason Series 14 figures hold their value a decade later.

Because the series retired long ago, Series 14 figures are now what collectors call a chase — you won't find them in a store, but they're easy to pick up as singles on BrickLink. Our take: start with the Witch, the Specter and the Skeleton Guy. They anchor almost every Halloween display.

The scattered classics: Halloween minifigs in other CMF series

LEGO has also tucked at least one Halloween figure into most of the main Collectible Minifigures series. These are the ones every collector should know:

  • Series 1 — Zombie. The one that started it: a shuffling green zombie in a torn suit, with a little lantern and a trans-neon bone. One of the most iconic early CMFs.
  • Series 2 — Classic Witch and Vampire. The pointed-hat witch with her black cat, and a tall-collared vampire with a bat. These two set the template for every Halloween LEGO figure since.
  • Series 4 — Werewolf and The Monster. A furry, snarling werewolf and a green Frankenstein-style monster. Both are centerpieces of a classic Halloween row.
  • Series 9 — Mummy. Wrapped in brown bandages, with a scarab, this one hides in a series that was mostly fantasy and sports.
  • Series 16 — Spooky Boy. The small kid in a ghost-sheet costume, holding a jack-o'-lantern bucket. Still one of the most-requested CMFs on our search log every October.

None of these come as singles anymore in stores, but all show up affordably on BrickLink. A full "greatest hits" row — Zombie, Witch, Vampire, Werewolf, Monster, Mummy, Spooky Boy — is often cheaper than a single current-year CMF case.

Beyond the CMF line: Monster Fighters and Hidden Side

Two LEGO themes went all-in on Halloween monsters in bigger sets:

Monster Fighters (2012). A short, deliberately Halloween-flavored theme with Lord Vampyre, the Mummy, Zombie Driver, a Swamp Creature and a cast of monster hunters. The sets — Haunted House (10228), Vampyre Castle (9468), Werewolf (9463) — have retired and gone up in value, but the minifigures remain affordable as singles. If you want "spooky but LEGO-cute," Monster Fighters is the gold mine.

Hidden Side (2019–2020). A later ghost-hunter theme with an AR app, filled with minifigures of possessed townspeople, graveyard spirits and classic haunted-house ghosts. The theme is retired but the figures are plentiful and inexpensive — a great way to add ghosts, specters and translucent-eyed townspeople to a Halloween display without hunting rare bags.

And every few years LEGO reissues the all-time hits in anniversary CMFs, so pieces of the Series 2 Witch, the Series 4 Werewolf and the Series 14 Zombie Cheerleader have come back in reprinted form — keep an eye on new CMF announcements each spring.

Build your own: the essential Halloween parts

Here is where a BrickLink store is more fun than a sealed blind bag. LEGO has molded a full catalog of spooky accessories that you can snap onto any standard torso and legs to build the Halloween character of your choice. The ones we restock every October:

  • Skeleton body. Molded white arms, torso and legs that assemble into a full skeleton minifig. Pair it with a jack-o'-lantern head and you have an instant classic.
  • Jack-o'-lantern head. Orange, printed with a carved face, fits on any neck stud. The single best Halloween part LEGO makes — drop it on a plain-robed figure and you have a scarecrow in two seconds.
  • Witch hat. The classic cone with a wide brim. Works for witches, wizards, scarecrows, half the Hogwarts cast.
  • Grim Reaper hood (cowl). A long black hood with cloak attachment. Add a bare skeleton head underneath and a scythe in hand and you are done.
  • Spider and bat. Small molded animals, usually black or dark-brown. Perfect scenery for a graveyard or belfry build.
  • Chain (16 links). The flexible plastic chain haunts every dungeon diorama. Drape it over a skeleton or a tombstone.
  • Candle with trans-orange flame. The flicker in a cold hand. Great for a specter, a monk, a lantern-lit scene.
  • Broom and cauldron. No witch is complete without them. The cauldron doubles as a cheap stew pot for a goblin camp.
  • Tombstone. A classic rounded gray slab. Line a few up with spiders and bats and you have a graveyard base plate.
  • Spider web (1x1 corner). Translucent white, clips onto any wall corner. Instant abandoned attic.

The whole kit above costs less than a single sealed CMF case, and you can make a half-dozen one-of-one Halloween characters out of parts you already have, plus a few new heads and accessories. How to collect without the duplicate pile

Here is the thing about Collectible Minifigures: they come in sealed blind bags, so when a series is current you don't know which figure you're getting until you open it. Buy five bags to chase the Witch and you'll get three Spider Ladies and no Witch, every time. Buy a full case to guarantee the set and you're well into three-figure territory, with a mountain of duplicates to resell.

The simple answer, especially for retired series like Series 14 and the early classics: buy the exact figure you want as a single. That is literally what a minifigure-focused BrickLink store exists for. You browse by name, pick the Witch, the Werewolf and the Spooky Boy, and we ship you only those. No lottery, no duplicates, no leftover Fly Monsters nobody wants.

A Halloween row for every budget

If you only have room for a short display shelf, here are three starting rows that work:

  • The classics ($$): Series 1 Zombie, Series 2 Witch, Series 2 Vampire, Series 4 Werewolf, Series 4 Monster. Five figures, the whole monster-movie canon.
  • The modern Halloween row ($$$): Series 14 Wacky Witch, Series 14 Specter, Series 14 Skeleton Guy, Series 14 Gargoyle, Series 16 Spooky Boy. Heavier on glow-in-the-dark and translucent pieces.
  • The custom row ($): One jack-o'-lantern head on a plain farmer, one skeleton body under a Grim Reaper hood, one witch hat on any wizard torso, one spider web on a tiny vignette. Four one-of-ones for the cost of a single rare figure.

Any of them makes a Halloween shelf your friends will stop and look at. All of them are built out of parts and figures that are easy to track down a few weeks before October 31 — which, as it happens, is right now.
