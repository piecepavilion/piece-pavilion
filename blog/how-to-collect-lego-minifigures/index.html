How to Start Collecting LEGO Minifigures: A Beginner's Guide

Piece Pavilion · June 5, 2026

The minifigure is the heart of LEGO. Since the first one stepped onto a baseplate in 1978, the LEGO Group has produced thousands of unique characters — from generic townsfolk to licensed heroes, blind-bag exclusives, and one-off promotional rarities. For a lot of collectors, the little yellow figures are the whole hobby.

And they make a great thing to collect: they're inexpensive to start with, they don't eat up shelf space the way large sets do, the variety is almost endless, and desirable figures tend to hold — or grow — their value. If you're just getting started, here's everything you need to know.

1. Know the Main Types of Minifigure

Not all minifigures are created (or priced) equally. Most fall into one of these groups:

  • Collectible Minifigures (CMF) — The blind-bag series LEGO releases under theme numbers (Series 1, Series 2, and so on, plus tie-ins like Disney, Marvel, and LEGO Movie series). Each series has a set lineup, and because you can't see what's inside the bag, completing a full series is a popular collecting goal.
  • Licensed / theme figures — Characters from Star Wars, Harry Potter, Marvel and DC Super Heroes, Ninjago, and other themes. These only come in sets, so a specific figure's value often tracks how expensive or rare its set was.
  • City & generic figures — Everyday characters — police, firefighters, builders, civilians. Individually inexpensive, but a great, affordable way to build a fun collection or stock up for play and dioramas.
  • Exclusives & promos — Figures given away at events, bundled with magazines, or sold only at specific stores. These are usually the rarest and most valuable — and the ones to be most careful about when buying.

2. Learn to Identify Any Figure

Every LEGO minifigure has a unique catalog ID, and learning to use them is the single most useful skill for a collector. On BrickLink — the world's largest LEGO marketplace and catalog — each figure has a code made of a short theme prefix plus a number. A few examples:

  • sw — Star Wars (e.g. sw1234)
  • hp — Harry Potter
  • sh — Super Heroes (Marvel & DC)
  • col — Collectible Minifigures series

Once you know a figure's ID, you can look up exactly which sets it appeared in, what accessories it should include, and what it typically sells for. If you're holding a figure and don't know what it is, you can search BrickLink's catalog by theme and browse the images, or use the printing and accessories to narrow it down. This is also how you confirm you're buying the exact version you want — many characters have several variants with slightly different printing across the years.

3. Pick a Focus (So You Don't Go Broke)

"Collect every minifigure" is a fast track to an overwhelming, expensive pile. The collectors who enjoy the hobby most — and spend the most wisely — usually give themselves a focus. A few popular approaches:

  • By theme — Only Star Wars, only Harry Potter, only Ninjago.
  • By character — Every version of a favorite character across the years (every Batman, every Luke Skywalker).
  • By series — Completing full Collectible Minifigure series, one at a time.
  • By era — Classic figures from a specific decade, or only the early "no-printing" smiley faces.

A focus gives your collection a shape, makes "what do I buy next" an easy question, and keeps your spending intentional.

4. Check Condition and Completeness

With minifigures, condition is everything — and the details are small enough to miss. Before you buy (or when you're valuing your own), check:

  • Printing wear — Face and torso printing can rub off with play. Sharp, complete printing is worth far more than a faded figure.
  • Yellowing — White and light-gray pieces can yellow with age and sun exposure. It's hard to reverse, so it directly affects value.
  • Cracks and stress marks — Look at necks, hips, and legs for cracking, especially on older figures.
  • Accessories — A figure is only "complete" with its correct hat, hair, weapon, or tool. A Star Wars figure missing its lightsaber, or a CMF missing its display stand and accessory, is worth less. The catalog ID tells you exactly what should be included.

5. Watch Out for Fakes

Because minifigures are popular and small, the market is full of counterfeits — especially for licensed and high-value characters. Bootleg figures are often sold as the real thing. Protect yourself:

  • Buy from reputable sellers with strong feedback and clear, in-hand photos.
  • Be suspicious of "rare" licensed figures priced well below the going rate.
  • Check the LEGO logo on the studs — genuine figures have "LEGO" stamped on each stud, and authentic printing is crisp and correctly aligned.
  • When in doubt, compare against the catalog image for that exact figure ID.

7. Store and Display Them Right

However you like to keep your collection, a couple of habits will keep your figures looking their best:

  • Keep them out of direct sunlight — UV is the main cause of yellowing and faded printing.
  • Bag loose accessories — Tiny hands, capes, and weapons are easy to lose. Keep spares in labeled zip bags.
  • Use display cases for favorites — Minifigure display frames and small acrylic cases keep dust off the figures you want on show.
  • Don't over-pose older figures — Repeatedly bending arms and legs on vintage figures with stress marks can turn a crack into a break.

Start small, pick a focus, learn to read the catalog, and buy from sellers you trust — do that, and minifigure collecting is one of the most rewarding (and affordable) corners of the LEGO hobby.
