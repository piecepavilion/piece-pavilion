LEGO UCS Millennium Falcon 75192 Is 'Retiring' Again. Why You Shouldn't Panic-Buy It

Piece Pavilion · November 14, 2026 · 4 min read

The UCS Millennium Falcon (75192) lists at $849.99. Sealed copies have averaged about $615.12 on the resale side over the last six months, according to Brickranker. A set that's supposedly vanishing in six weeks is trading roughly $235 under retail. That's not what a real retirement scramble looks like.

Brickfact has the set tagged "Retiring Soon" for December 2026, and Brick Fanatics has pointed at it leaving this year too. So the panic posts are writing themselves. Here's why I'd breathe first.

This set has cried wolf for years

The Falcon launched in 2017 and was originally thought to be retiring in 2020. It didn't. Brick Fanatics has run multiple stories on it, including one headlined "retirement postponed yet again." At this point the retirement date is more of a seasonal tradition than a deadline.

That doesn't mean it can't go this December. It means a date on a tracker is a guess. LEGO doesn't announce exact retirement dates, and Brickfact says as much on its own pages.

The price tells you what the market believes

Retiring sets usually sell out of discounts first, then get pricey. Right now the Falcon is doing the opposite. Brickfact's price comparison showed a low of £570.99 including shipping on eBay as of November 6, which is 22% below the £734.99 UK retail price. The US numbers tell the same story: $849.99 RRP against that $615.12 sealed average.

Resale buyers aren't bidding it up in a rush. Either they don't believe the date, or the sheer size of the box (7,541 pieces, per Brick Fanatics' review) keeps the buyer pool small. Probably both.

What a real retirement would change

If LEGO does pull it, expect the usual pattern: the cheap copies dry up, and the sealed price drifts upward over the following year or two. I won't put a number on that, because nobody has one. The 2017 launch price was $799.99, so the set has only gone up $50 at retail in nine years. That's a slow-moving set.

One more wrinkle if you're parting it out or buying to build: sources disagree on the minifigure count. Brick Fanatics' review says 7, Brickfact lists 8. Check the box contents before you pay a premium for a specific figure.

Who should actually buy now

  • You want to build it. Buy it whenever a sale lines up. Black Friday is the obvious window.
  • You're buying as an investment. You're betting on a retirement LEGO has already postponed more than once. Keep that in mind.
  • You're waiting for a bargain. The current gap between retail and resale suggests you may not need to wait for anything.

If it's on your list, the real deadline is your own, not LEGO's. If it retires, you'll know. If it doesn't, you didn't overpay.
